# 2026-11-10 — Problems and threats (Lesson 27)

## Quick review（Lesson 26）

1. **stem from** — 源自……；由……引起
2. **set off a chain reaction** — 引發一連串互相關連嘅變化
3. **pave the way for** — 為……鋪路；創造條件令……更容易發生

## Today's 5 collocations

### 1. pose a threat to
- Weak → better: *be dangerous for / can harm* → **pose a threat to**
- 意思：對……構成威脅
- Frame: `pose a (serious / growing) threat to + noun`
- Model: Impersonation websites **pose a serious threat to** consumer confidence in digital banking.
- Common mistake: ~~pose a threat for public safety~~（後面接對象時用介詞 *to*，唔係 *for*）

### 2. be vulnerable to
- Weak → better: *be easily hurt by / be easily tricked by* → **be vulnerable to**
- 意思：容易受到……傷害／影響（較弱勢或防範不足）
- Frame: `be (particularly) vulnerable to + noun / -ing`
- Model: Elderly residents living alone **are particularly vulnerable to** telephone and online deception.
- Common mistake: ~~be vulnerable of scams~~（固定搭配係 *vulnerable to*）

### 3. fall victim to
- Weak → better: *get tricked by / suffer because of* → **fall victim to**
- 意思：成為……嘅受害者；不幸中招／受騙
- Frame: `fall victim to + crime / fraud / problem`
- Model: Even cautious shoppers can **fall victim to** phishing links that imitate real payment pages.
- Common mistake: ~~fall a victim of~~（固定動詞片語係 *fall victim to*，唔加 *a*，介詞用 *to*；若用名詞結構先寫 *become a victim of*）

### 4. run the risk of
- Weak → better: *might have / have a chance of suffering* → **run the risk of**
- 意思：冒住……嘅風險（後果通常係負面）
- Frame: `run the risk of + -ing / noun`
- Model: Users who enter one-time passwords on unverified pages **run the risk of** losing their savings.
- Common mistake: ~~run the risk to lose money~~（*of* 後面一定要接名詞或 *-ing*，唔接 *to + verb*）

### 5. compound the problem
- Weak → better: *make the problem even worse* → **compound the problem**
- 意思：令問題雪上加霜；進一步加劇原有問題
- Frame: `compound the problem (of + noun) / be compounded by + noun`
- Model: Counterfeit department logos **compound the problem** by making fake notices look official.
- Common mistake: ~~compound to the problem~~（*compound* 係及物動詞，直接接 *the problem*，唔使加 *to*）

## Mini drill

**A.** 填空：Users who disclose one-time passwords ________ ________ ________ ________ immediate financial loss.
**B.** 選正確介詞：Even cautious citizens may fall victim (**to** / of) sophisticated phishing websites.
**C.** 改錯：Fake government logos compound to the problem of online fraud.

## Reading passage

On 9 November, separate alerts from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), Hong Kong Interbank Clearing Limited (HKICL) and the Environmental Protection Department (EPD) highlighted how impersonation scams continue to **pose a threat to** digital payments and public trust. Fraudsters have created bogus bank login screens, a fake Faster Payment System (FPS) "Buyer Online Protection" website, and posters for a fabricated "Renewable Resource Recovery Incentive Scheme". Because these schemes mimic everyday banking and recycling services, residents **are vulnerable to** misleading claims and may **fall victim to** phishing. Anyone who enters banking credentials or one-time passwords **runs the risk of** immediate financial loss. Counterfeit department logos and fake customer-service agents **compound the problem** by making scams harder to detect, prompting authorities to remind citizens never to share sensitive information via unknown links.

**Passage tasks**
1. Which three Hong Kong public bodies issued scam alerts on 9 November, and what fake schemes or websites were involved?
2. According to the passage, why do counterfeit logos and fake customer-service agents compound the problem?
3. True / False：The HKMA and the EPD sometimes ask citizens to submit banking credentials or one-time passwords through embedded SMS links.

## ANSWER KEY

### Mini drill
- A: **run the risk of**
- B: **to**
- C: Fake government logos **compound the problem** of online fraud.（*compound* 直接接賓語，唔加 *to*）

### Passage tasks
1. The **HKMA** (bogus bank login screens / apps), **HKICL** (a fake FPS "Buyer Online Protection" website), and the **EPD** (posters for a fabricated "Renewable Resource Recovery Incentive Scheme").
2. Because they make the scams look official and harder for the public to detect.
3. **False**（Authorities remind citizens never to share sensitive information via unknown links; banks and departments do not ask for passwords or one-time codes via embedded links.）
