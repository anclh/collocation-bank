# 2026-11-10 — Problems and threats (Lesson 27)

## Quick review（Lesson 26）

1. **stem from** — 源自……；由……引起
2. **set off a chain reaction** — 引發一連串互相關連嘅變化
3. **pave the way for** — 為……鋪路；創造條件令……更容易發生

## Today's 5 collocations

### 1. be fraught with danger
- Weak → better: *be full of danger / have many risks* → **be fraught with danger**
- 意思：充滿危險／危機四伏（亦常見 `be fraught with risks / difficulties`）
- Frame: `be fraught with + danger / risks / difficulties`
- Model: Clicking on unverified payment links **is fraught with danger** for online shoppers.
- Common mistake: ~~be fraught of danger~~（固定搭配介詞係 *with*，唔係 *of*）

### 2. expose someone to the risk of
- Weak → better: *put someone in danger of* → **expose someone to the risk of**
- 意思：令某人暴露於……嘅風險之中
- Frame: `expose + person / group + to the risk of + noun / -ing`
- Model: Sharing one-time passwords **exposes customers to the risk of** unauthorized bank transfers.
- Common mistake: ~~expose customers with the risk~~（固定結構係 *expose sb to sth*，介詞用 *to*）

### 3. fall victim to
- Weak → better: *get tricked by / suffer because of* → **fall victim to**
- 意思：成為……嘅受害者；不幸中招／受騙
- Frame: `fall victim to + crime / fraud / problem`
- Model: Even cautious shoppers can **fall victim to** phishing links that imitate real payment pages.
- Common mistake: ~~fall a victim of~~（固定動詞片語係 *fall victim to*，唔加 *a*，介詞用 *to*；若用名詞結構先寫 *become a victim of*）

### 4. compound the problem
- Weak → better: *make the problem even worse* → **compound the problem**
- 意思：令問題雪上加霜；進一步加劇原有問題
- Frame: `compound the problem (of + noun) / be compounded by + noun`
- Model: Counterfeit department logos **compound the problem** by making fake notices look official.
- Common mistake: ~~compound to the problem~~（*compound* 係及物動詞，直接接 *the problem*，唔使加 *to*）

### 5. undermine public trust in
- Weak → better: *make people trust ... less* → **undermine public trust in**
- 意思：削弱／動搖公眾對……嘅信任
- Frame: `undermine (public) trust / confidence in + institution / system`
- Model: Rampant impersonation scams can **undermine public trust in** digital payment platforms and government notices.
- Common mistake: ~~undermine public trust on~~（*trust* 或 *confidence* 後面接對象時用介詞 *in*，唔係 *on*）

## Mini drill

**A.** 填空：Clicking on unverified SMS hyperlinks ________ ________ ________ ________ for banking customers.
**B.** 選正確介詞：Rampant impersonation scams can undermine public trust (**in** / **on**) digital payment systems.
**C.** 改錯：Fake government logos compound to the problem of online fraud.

## Reading passage

On 9 November, separate alerts from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), Hong Kong Interbank Clearing Limited (HKICL) and the Environmental Protection Department (EPD) highlighted how responding to unverified online links **is fraught with danger**. Fraudsters have created bogus bank login screens, a fake Faster Payment System (FPS) "Buyer Online Protection" website, and posters for a fabricated "Renewable Resource Recovery Incentive Scheme". Because these schemes mimic everyday banking and recycling services, even attentive residents may **fall victim to** phishing. Disclosing login credentials or one-time passwords **exposes users to the risk of** immediate financial loss. Counterfeit department logos and fake customer-service agents **compound the problem** by making scams harder to detect, while repeated impersonation of public bodies can **undermine public trust in** genuine digital services and green subsidies.

**Passage tasks**
1. Which three Hong Kong public bodies issued scam alerts on 9 November, and what fake schemes or websites were involved?
2. According to the passage, why do counterfeit logos and fake customer-service agents compound the problem?
3. True / False：The HKMA and the EPD sometimes ask citizens to submit banking credentials or one-time passwords through embedded SMS links.

## ANSWER KEY

### Mini drill
- A: **is fraught with danger**
- B: **in**
- C: Fake government logos **compound the problem** of online fraud.（*compound* 直接接賓語，唔加 *to*）

### Passage tasks
1. The **HKMA** (bogus bank login screens / apps), **HKICL** (a fake FPS "Buyer Online Protection" website), and the **EPD** (posters for a fabricated "Renewable Resource Recovery Incentive Scheme").
2. Because they make the scams look official and harder for the public to detect.
3. **False**（Authorities remind citizens never to share sensitive information via unknown links; banks and departments do not ask for passwords or one-time codes via embedded links.）
