# 2026-10-15 — Education (Lesson 19)

## Quick review（Lesson 18）

1. **take the stance that** — 採取某一立場
2. **put forward the argument that** — 提出論點
3. **make a compelling case for** — 提出有力理據支持

## Today's 5 collocations

### 1. acquire essential skills
- Weak → better: *learn useful stuff* → **acquire essential skills**
- 意思：掌握關鍵／必需技能
- Frame: `acquire essential skills (in / such as …)`
- Model: Vocational courses help students **acquire essential skills** for the workplace.
- Common mistake: academic writing 常用 *acquire … skills*

### 2. drop out of school
- Weak → better: *leave school early / quit school* → **drop out of school**
- 意思：輟學（中途離校）
- Frame: `drop out of school / university / college`
- Model: Students who lack support are more likely to **drop out of school**.
- Common mistake: *drop out of*，唔係 *drop out from*

### 3. receive a well-rounded education
- Weak → better: *learn many different subjects* → **receive a well-rounded education**
- 意思：接受全面／均衡教育
- Frame: `receive a well-rounded education`
- Model: Parents want their children to **receive a well-rounded education**, not only exam drill.
- Common mistake: *well-rounded*（有 hyphen）

### 4. gain admission to
- Weak → better: *get into (a uni)* → **gain admission to**
- 意思：獲錄取進入（學校／課程）
- Frame: `gain admission to + university / programme / course`
- Model: Competition to **gain admission to** top universities remains intense.
- Common mistake: 正式文多用 *gain admission to*

### 5. undertake vocational training
- Weak → better: *do job training / learn a trade* → **undertake vocational training**
- 意思：接受職業培訓
- Frame: `undertake vocational training (in …)`
- Model: More young people now **undertake vocational training** instead of a traditional degree.
- Common mistake: *undertake* + training／study 係學術常用

## Reading passage

Many young people feel pressure to **gain admission to** a famous university, yet academic success alone is not enough. Schools that help students **acquire essential skills** — such as communication, problem-solving and teamwork — prepare them better for work and life. At the same time, not every student thrives in a purely academic track. Those who **undertake vocational training** can build practical expertise and still enjoy strong career prospects. The goal should be that every learner can **receive a well-rounded education**, rather than being pushed into a single path that does not fit them. When support is limited, some students **drop out of school** early, which often narrows their options later. Balanced pathways matter more than prestige alone.
